# Value-first customer reply

Source of truth: the Customer Workshop Playbook Google Doc in the business Drive (client workshops folder), stages 1 to 3. When this process changes, update both the playbook and this skill, and add a row to the playbook's Lessons learned log.

We sell an outcome, the person the customer becomes, not a list of topics. The workshop is the vehicle. The agenda still exists and gets refined, but customer-facing replies lead with value.

## Which reply is this?
- **First reply (lead stage):** lead with the outcome and book a discovery call. No price yet.
- **Proposal (after the discovery call):** outcomes shaped by what you learned, then price, terms and next step.

## First reply: steps
1. **Verify the sender** is legitimate before engaging with any inbound offer.
2. **Answer their logistics in one line, then move on.** Confirm what they proposed and ask the one question that moves things forward. Don't thank them for agreeing to the format.
3. **Say what they'll walk away with.** 3 to 5 outcomes, not topics:
   - The big picture (the landscape, what's realistic), so they can judge what's worth paying for.
   - A working result built for their real use case.
   - Connecting it to the systems they already use, and a plan for their team afterward.
   - Fold supporting topics (security, evaluation) into an outcome ("built in as we set it up").
4. **Promise the shape, not the syllabus.** "I'll shape each session around getting you those results."
5. **Ask how they found us** in one light line ("And how did you hear about us?"). Save the full set of up to 5 attribution questions for the discovery call (playbook stage 2, "How they found us"): exact channel, what triggered the search, what they searched for or considered, why they reached out, and where peers like them learn about tools. The answers steer ad budget toward more leaders and small business owners like them.
6. **Close by booking the discovery call.** For example: "Could we do a 30-minute call this week? Here are a few times." No price in this email.

## Proposal: steps (after the discovery call)
1. Restate the outcomes, shaped by the call.
2. **Price honestly.** The normal price, any discount with a clear named reason (for example, a nonprofit discount; usually 10 to 30%), the final total, payment terms, and what's not included (such as the client's own AI tool subscription). Make sure the discount is real against the list price. Hold firm once stated.
3. **One-line close tied to the next step.** For example: "Once we lock in your ideal start date, I'll send a Stripe invoice."
4. **Keep it to one email** so they can evaluate logistics, value, price and next step at once.

## Checks before drafting
- Every bullet answers "what do they get or become?", not "what will we cover?"
- No price in a first reply.
- No hedging, no over-explaining, no restating their message.
- Plain, warm, confident tone; short sentences; no em dashes.
- Draft for the owner's review. Never send without approval.

## Example first reply
> Every other week works well for me.
>
> Here's what you'll walk away with after our time together:
> - A clear big-picture view of the AI landscape, so you can judge which tools are actually worth paying for.
> - A working agent built for a real use case, with evaluation and security built in as we set it up.
> - An understanding of how to connect it to the systems your team already uses.
> - A plan for rolling this out to your team afterward.
>
> I'll shape each session around getting you those results.
>
> Could we do a 30-minute call this week so I can learn about your team and tools? And how did you hear about us?

## Example proposal close
> The sessions are normally [list price]. With our [named discount], your total is [final total], [payment terms]. [What's not included.]
>
> Once we lock in your ideal start date, I'll send a Stripe invoice.
